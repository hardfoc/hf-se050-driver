Hardware¶

What to connect between your MCU and an SE050, with which bus settings, and the board-level details that decide whether the chip answers.

All values here come from the SE050 datasheet (Rev. 3.8, 18 October 2023) unless a row says otherwise. NXP does not allow its PDFs to be redistributed, so they are linked, not stored in the repository.

Pins (HX2QFN20)¶

Pin Name Use with this driver
9 I2C_SDA I2C data, open drain
10 I2C_SCL I2C clock, input
11 ENA Deep power-down while low
12 VIN I2C pad supply
18 VCC Logic supply
15 VOUT Feeds VCC for deep power-down
19 VSS Ground
3, 16 IO1, IO2 Not used
13, 14 CLK, RST_N Not used
1, 17 LB, LA Not used: to VSS
2, 4-8, 20 n.c. Not connected
  • ENA is referenced to VIN. Tie it to VCC when you do not drive it.
  • VIN also supplies the logic when deep power-down is used; VCC is then fed from VOUT on the board. Without deep power-down, leave VOUT unconnected.
  • IO1 / IO2 carry ISO 7816 or the SE050's own I2C controller (400 kHz) for sensors behind it. CLK / RST_N belong to ISO 7816; RST_N has no effect when only I2C is enabled. LA / LB are the ISO 14443 antenna.
  • Connect the centre pad to ground for heat.

Reset and power modes¶

There is no reset pin for the I2C interface. A board resets the SE050 by power-cycling it, or with ENA:

Mode Enter Leave
Deep power-down: < 5 µA, state lost ENA low ENA high, then boot
Power-down: < 500 µA, state kept "End of APDU session" S-block Falling edge on SDA

Deep power-down needs the chip supplied through VIN with VCC fed from VOUT. Wired that way, the port's HardwareReset() can pulse ENA low and high to give a clean start; the reference port holds it low for 2 ms and waits 10 ms after release. With VCC tied straight to the supply, use a load switch on the supply instead. The driver does not send the end-of-session block, so it never enters power-down mode by itself.

Keep SDA and SCL high while the SE050 boots

At power-up the SE050 picks its interface: it uses I2C only when I2C_SDA and I2C_SCL are both high (its weak internal pull-ups are on). A host that holds the bus low during the SE050's boot leaves it waiting for another interface until the next reset.

Bus settings¶

Setting Value
Role I2C target; the host is the controller
Address 0x48 (7-bit, 1001000), se050::kDefaultI2cAddress7
Speed Up to 3.4 MHz (high-speed mode) with clock stretching; 1 MHz without
Clock stretching On by default in configurations A, B, C, D, F, above 600 kHz; off by default on SE050E
Pull-ups External, to VIN, sized for the bus speed and capacitance
Protocol Smartcard T=1 over I2C, NXP UM11225 (T=1 over I2C)

A host that cannot handle clock stretching stays below 600 kHz on a configuration with stretching on, or uses a configuration with stretching off, which runs up to 1 MHz. The ESP32 examples run at 100 kHz, which every configuration supports; start there for bring-up.

How an exchange looks on the bus¶

The host writes one complete T=1 block, waits the inter-frame delay, then reads the answer in two parts: the three-byte prologue (NAD, PCB, LEN), then LEN bytes of information and the two-byte CRC. While the SE050 is still working it NACKs its address, and the driver retries the read.

sequenceDiagram
    participant H as Host (driver)
    participant S as SE050
    H->>S: write: 5A PCB LEN [APDU ...] CRC
    Note over H: inter-frame delay (2 ms)
    H->>S: read 3 bytes
    S--xH: NACK (busy)
    Note over H: retry after 2 ms
    H->>S: read 3 bytes
    S-->>H: A5 PCB LEN
    H->>S: read LEN + 2 bytes
    S-->>H: [R-APDU ...] CRC

A long operation (key generation, RSA) may answer with an S(WTX) request instead; the driver answers it and keeps reading. T=1 over I2C has the block types and the timing settings.

Power¶

Supply Range Notes
VIN, VCC 1.62 V to 3.6 V, 1.8 V typical Characterised range. 6 V absolute maximum; above 3.6 V the deep power-down current is not guaranteed
Digital inputs −0.3 V to VIN + 0.3 V SDA, SCL and ENA are referenced to VIN
Temperature grade (part number digit) Ambient range
1 (standard) −25 °C to 85 °C
2 (extended) −40 °C to 105 °C

Run the MCU's I/O at the SE050's VIN, or add a level shifter: the SE050 is not 5 V tolerant within its operating range.

Errata that affect the host¶

From the SE050 errata sheet (Rev. 1.0, 14 October 2020):

  • I2C.1, lock-up on an erroneous APDU sequence. A command written before the previous response was read in full can make the SE050 stretch SCL forever (when clock stretching is on), until it is power-cycled. A host that resets in the middle of an exchange causes exactly this. The driver always reads a response to the end; after a host reset, follow NXP's workaround (3.1.3) and read from the SE050 before the first command: T1().RecoverAfterHostReset() reads any pending answer and resynchronises. Once the bus is locked only a power cycle helps: wire ENA so HardwareReset() can do it.
  • I2C.2, empty frame. An acknowledged address-only frame (START, address, STOP) also makes the SE050 stretch the clock forever. The driver never sends one and Session::TransceiveRaw() rejects a zero-length write. Do not run an I2C bus scan with the SE050 on the bus.
  • I2C.3, lines low at start-up. SDA or SCL low during interface detection triggers a security reset after about 1 s, and repeated events lock the chip. Keep the bus idle high, with pull-ups, whenever the SE050 is powered.
  • I2C.4, edge timing. SCL must change before SDA with a margin: data hold time (50 % to 50 %) at least 8 ns for a falling and 24 ns for a rising SDA edge in fast mode. Check your I2C controller's hold-time setting.
  • JOS.2, SCP03 counter. Inside a platform SCP03 session, a command without command data does not advance the SE050's encryption counter, and the next command fails. The driver applies NXP's workaround (3.13.3): every wrapped command carries encrypted data. See Security model.

One host per SE050, one command at a time

I2C.1 is why the driver is not thread-safe and must not be shared between tasks without a lock: two commands interleaved on the bus can hang it until a power cycle.

Configurations¶

SE050 is a family of pin-compatible configurations (SE050A, B, C, D, E, F and their variants) that differ in enabled features, pre-provisioned credentials, clock-stretching default and certification. NXP's configurations note AN12436 lists them; check the one on your board before you choose a bus speed, an applet AID or object identifiers. The default AID in se050::applet::kDefaultIoTAppletAid is the Plug & Trust IoT applet's; confirm it for your configuration.

Evaluation and example boards¶

The ESP32 examples run on an ESP32-S3 with the SE050 on I2C port 0. The fields are those of Se050EspI2cConfig; ENA is not connected by default (GPIO_NUM_NC).

Signal ESP32-S3 Field
SDA 47 sda
SCL 48 scl
ENA n.c. reset_gpio
Bus speed 100 kHz freq_hz
Address 0x48 device_address7

The reference port turns on the ESP32's internal pull-ups, which are weak; add external pull-ups for anything but a short bench connection. The configuration is in hf_se050_esp_i2c.hpp.
